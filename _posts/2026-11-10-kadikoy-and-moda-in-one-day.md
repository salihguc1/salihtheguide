If you have one day for Istanbul’s Asian side, I’d take the ferry to Kadıköy and spend it between the market and Moda. You can start with tea by the pier, eat lunch in the market and end beside the Sea of Marmara. There’s enough to do in these few streets that I’d give them the whole day before adding another neighbourhood.

## Morning: take the ferry to Kadıköy

If you’re staying in the Old City, look up a **Kadıköy** ferry from Eminönü. Karaköy is another departure point if you’re coming from Galata. Check the [Şehir Hatları timetable](https://sehirhatlari.istanbul/en/timetables) for the exact pier, direction and sailing on your date. There are several routes at the waterfront, so read the destination on the sign before tapping in.

If you arrive in Kadıköy by metro instead, use the waterfront as your starting point. Take a minute to see where the ferries leave from, especially if you plan to go back by boat. Different routes may use different boarding points. Save the return pier as a pin along with your hotel.

## Walk into the market

From the ferry area, head inland to **Kadıköy Çarşı**. The market is a set of streets rather than a single building. Around **Güneşlibahçe Sokak**, look for fishmongers, pickle shops, counters of olives and cheeses, and bakeries with trays in the window.

I’d get a small breakfast before you start browsing, then come back for a proper lunch when you’ve seen the options. A **simit** with tea is enough if you plan to eat early. A slice of **börek** works if you want something warmer. At a counter, *Peynirli börek var mı?* means “Do you have cheese börek?”

Leave a little room in your bag if you like bringing home food. A small packet of tea or a bag of spices is easier to carry than a jar of pickles. If you want olives or cheese, ask whether the shop can pack them securely and check what your destination allows you to bring in before buying.

## Lunch: look for a lokanta

A **lokanta** with trays of cooked food makes lunch easy when everyone wants something different. You can look at the dishes before ordering. Try a bowl of **mercimek çorbası** (lentil soup), or pick a vegetable dish and add rice or yoghurt. *Bugün ne var?* means “What do you have today?”

If you’d prefer a fish meal, look at the menu before you sit down and ask how the fish is priced. A price per portion is easy to compare. If the price is by weight, ask what the portion you’re ordering will cost. I’d leave time to sit down for this meal, then save the next tea for Moda.

## Afternoon: follow Bahariye towards Moda

From the market, go towards **Altıyol** and the **Bull Statue**. This is a handy meeting point if your group splits up. From there, follow **Bahariye Caddesi** past the **Süreyya Opera House**. Look at the façade from the street. If a performance interests you, treat it as a separate plan and check the programme before coming.

You’ll see tracks for the [T3 Kadıköy–Moda heritage tram](https://www.metro.istanbul/en/Hatlarimiz/HatDetay?hat=T3) along parts of this walk. It runs in a loop, so check which way it’s going and where it stops. I’d walk this stretch if I wanted to browse the shops, and consider the tram if my feet needed a break.

Continue towards **Moda Caddesi** and pick a side street for a coffee stop. If you’re curious about Turkish music, save **Barış Manço Evi** as an optional stop and check the museum’s hours before planning an interior visit. The walk works well even if you only see the house from outside.

## Late afternoon: walk down to the water

Head towards **Moda İskelesi** or **Moda Sahili**. The pier and the waterfront path make good targets, but the final slope varies depending on which street you take. If hills or stairs are a concern, check the walking route before leaving the high street and give yourself time for a gentler approach.

Once you’re down by the water, find a place to sit. Take a jacket if there’s a breeze, even if the market felt warm. This is where I’d leave the afternoon loose. If the weather’s good, keep walking along the shore. If it turns, go back towards Moda Caddesi for tea or ice cream.

## Evening: return to the pier or stay for dinner

If you want to eat on the Asian side, head back towards the market and choose dinner before returning to the ferry. The walk is more enjoyable when you know which departures still work for you. Check both the last sailing and a backup while you still have time to change plans.

For a trip built around food, my [Istanbul street food guide](/blog/istanbul-street-foods/) gives you a few more things to order. If you’d like to fit this area into a first visit, see how I combine a ferry and Kadıköy in my [three-day Istanbul itinerary](/blog/istanbul-in-three-days/).

If you’ve bought something to take back, make the market your last stop so you don’t have to carry it all the way to Moda and back. Keep that errand separate from the ferry deadline.

## My tip

Pick one thing to do in Moda that can take as long as you like. For me, I’d leave that time for the waterfront. If you spend longer in the market than planned, you can still get down to the sea for a short walk and finish the day where you wanted to.
