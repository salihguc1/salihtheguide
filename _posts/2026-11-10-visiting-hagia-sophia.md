When someone asks me how to visit Hagia Sophia, I start with the entrances. Hagia Sophia is an active mosque, and the ticketed sightseeing route is separate from the entrance for worship. If you’re coming to see the building and its mosaics, look for the **Visitor Gallery** signs on the side facing Topkapı Palace’s Imperial Gate.

## Find the visitor entrance in Sultanahmet

From Sultanahmet’s T1 tram stop, walk towards the square between Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque. Continue towards the **Fountain of Ahmed III** and **Bab-ı Hümayun**, the gate into Topkapı Palace’s outer courtyard. The ticketed visitor approach is on this side of Hagia Sophia. If fencing or restoration has shifted the queue, follow the posted directions.

This is where I’d stop and check what the queue is for. Several entrances and ticket lines are close together, and the first line you see might be for something else. If you need to ask, *Ayasofya ziyaretçi girişi nerede?* means “Where is the Hagia Sophia visitor entrance?”

## What a gallery ticket lets you see

The sightseeing route takes you through the upper gallery. From there, you can look across the space below the dome and see mosaics along the visitor route, subject to access and restoration. The worship area follows its own access arrangements. If a ticket listing describes a “mosque visit,” read the details so you know which part of the building is included.

There’s also a separate Hagia Sophia history museum nearby. If you see a combined ticket, check which venues it covers and whether you want both. For a first visit with a full day planned in Sultanahmet, I’d make time for the building itself, then decide whether I want the extra museum visit.

## What I’d look at inside

### The space under the dome

Find a clear spot on the gallery route and look across before you take your first photo. Notice how the columns and arches frame the view, then look up at the ring of windows around the base of the dome. As you move, the angle changes enough to make the room look different from one side of the gallery to the other.

### The mosaics

If the **Deësis mosaic** is visible along the route, stop long enough to look at the faces and the shades of gold around them. The surviving figures are Christ, Mary and John the Baptist. Look for the detail in the eyes and the way the small pieces catch the light before you move on.

Some parts of Hagia Sophia may be screened off or covered during conservation work. I’d treat any specific mosaic as a bonus and leave time for what you can see. If you’ve been looking at photographs before the trip, remember that they may show an angle from a different level or from before the current visitor route was introduced.

## What to wear and when to go

I’d wear something that covers the shoulders and knees and bring a light scarf if you’ll need a head covering. Follow the instructions at the entrance on footwear, dress and photos. Keep your voice low, leave room for others to pass, and avoid photographing people at prayer.

Check the [Hagia Sophia visiting information](https://hagiasophiamuseums.org/en/visit) for current ticket prices, entrance details and any changes to access. If you’re visiting on a Friday, check prayer-related arrangements separately for Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque. Give yourself room to move things around on the day, especially if you’ve booked timed entry elsewhere.

The gallery approach and the historic surfaces can be difficult if you have limited mobility. Ask about the current accessible route before buying a ticket, including how you get to the upper level and how you exit. If you’re visiting with a pushchair, ask what the entrance staff recommend for the route that’s open that day.

## What to see nearby

### Blue Mosque and Hippodrome

The Blue Mosque is across the square. It has its own visitor entrance and prayer breaks. I’d visit whichever building fits your timing first and use the old Hippodrome as a flexible stop in between. Look for the Egyptian Obelisk and the Serpent Column along the square. You can see both from outside.

### Basilica Cistern

The Basilica Cistern is a short walk away and has a separate ticket. Going underground after looking up at Hagia Sophia’s dome makes a good change of pace. If you’re going to both, I’d put lunch or a tea break between them so the second visit gets your full attention.

### Topkapı Palace

The palace gate near the gallery entrance is useful as a landmark. The palace visit itself takes longer, especially if you want to see several courtyards and the Harem. Give it its own part of the day. If you’re choosing a combined palace ticket, check what’s included on the [National Palaces ticket page](https://www.millisaraylar.gov.tr/bilet/biletal?culture=en).

## How I’d put the morning together

I’d start in Sultanahmet with the building whose admission suits my arrival time, take a walk across the Hippodrome, then use the other mosque or the cistern as my next stop. Check which ticket you can buy on the day and which one you want to reserve. Avoid arranging two timed entries so close together that one security queue could make you miss the other.

## My tip

Before you leave for Sultanahmet, save the **Fountain of Ahmed III** as a map pin. It gives you a more precise landmark for the Hagia Sophia visitor entrance than the middle of the square, and it’s easy to ask for if you need directions.
