The first thing I check when someone asks how to get from an Istanbul airport to the city centre is the three-letter code on their ticket. **IST** is Istanbul Airport, on the European side. **SAW** is Sabiha Gökçen, on the Asian side. From there, I look up the hotel on a map, since “central Istanbul” could mean Sultanahmet, Taksim or Kadıköy.

## Pick the route by neighbourhood

- **IST to Taksim:** Take the M11 to Gayrettepe and change to the M2 towards Yenikapı. Get off at Taksim. A Havaist shuttle is another option if its Taksim stop suits your hotel.
- **IST to Sultanahmet:** Take the M11 and M2 to Taksim, then the F1 down to Kabataş and the T1 tram to Sultanahmet. For a bus option, compare the current Havaist stop with your hotel address before deciding.
- **SAW to Kadıköy:** The M4 goes to Kadıköy. Havabüs also runs to Kadıköy, which can be convenient if you prefer loading your bag once and sitting down.
- **SAW to Taksim or Sultanahmet:** Look at the direct Havabüs service to Taksim if you're staying near the square. For Sultanahmet, compare an M4 and Marmaray trip with a taxi or transfer, especially if you're arriving after dark with several bags.

## From Istanbul Airport (IST)

### The metro via Gayrettepe

Follow the metro signs after arrivals and take the M11 in the direction of Gayrettepe. At Gayrettepe, follow the signs for the M2 and take a Yenikapı-bound train. Use Taksim for the square and upper İstiklal Avenue, or Şişhane if your hotel is nearer Tünel or Galata. Check which station exit is closest before you go above ground.

Give yourself time for the airport walk and the Gayrettepe transfer. It can feel longer than the route looks on a diagram, especially with luggage. If you’re continuing to Sultanahmet via Kabataş, budget for each interchange and check that the F1 and T1 are still running when you reach them.

### The Havaist shuttle

Look up your destination on [Havaist](https://www.hava.ist/) before leaving the terminal. Check the stop pin as well as the name. If your hotel is on the other side of Taksim Square or down towards Cihangir, the last stretch can involve a hill or a short taxi ride.

Havaist has its own fares and payment arrangements. Check those on the operator’s site, even if you already have an İstanbulkart for the metro and tram. I’d choose the shuttle if the stop is a short walk from the hotel and I can avoid several changes with a suitcase.

## From Sabiha Gökçen (SAW)

### The M4 to Kadıköy and Marmaray

The [M4 airport metro](https://www.metro.istanbul/en/Hatlarimiz/HatDetay?hat=M4) runs to Kadıköy. If you’re staying in Moda, check how far your hotel is from the final station. The waterfront and the streets around Bahariye sit at different levels, so a short distance on a map can still mean pushing a suitcase uphill.

For the Old City, change at Ayrılık Çeşmesi for Marmaray towards the European side and get off at Sirkeci. From there, walk or take the T1 towards Sultanahmet, depending on your hotel. Marmaray runs on a separate timetable. Check it as well as the M4 if your flight gets in late.

### The Havabüs shuttle

[Havabüs](https://www.havabus.com/?lang=tr) lists services from Sabiha Gökçen to Taksim and Kadıköy. Check the airport-to-city departure column and the stop map. The time listed for the reverse journey can be different, even though both rows name the same places.

If you’re thinking of continuing from Kadıköy by ferry, look up the onward sailing first. With bags and a long flight behind you, a ferry is a pleasant option only when the pier and schedule fit. A Marmaray connection may suit you better if your hotel is near Sirkeci.

## When I’d take a taxi or arrange a transfer

A taxi or hotel transfer makes sense after the last useful connection, with heavy bags, or when several travellers are sharing the cost. Use the official taxi rank at the terminal, or book with a provider that gives you written pickup instructions. Have your hotel’s full address and a map pin ready. A hotel name by itself can send you to the wrong branch.

For a taxi, confirm that the meter is running and ask about any tolls for the proposed route. For a prebooked transfer, ask for the total price and whether tolls and waiting time are included. It helps to take a screenshot of the price, the meeting point and the driver’s contact details before boarding your flight.

## Before you fly

Check the first and last departures for every leg you plan to use. Allow time for passport control and baggage claim. If your flight lands near the last train, check the options for the time you’re likely to leave arrivals, then save a backup route.

On the return trip, check the airport code again. If you're changing airports between flights, plan the crossing separately and give yourself plenty of time. ## My tip

Ask the hotel which station exit or bus stop they’d use with luggage. It’s a small question that can save you from dragging your bags up a hill or getting off at a stop that sounds closer than it is. Save their answer and the hotel address before you leave home.
